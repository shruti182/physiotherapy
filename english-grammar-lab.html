English Grammar Lab

Five grammar rules turned into instant transformers. Fill in the blanks, and watch the rule do the work — no memorising required.

Active → Passive Voice Subject ↔ Object

The object of the active sentence becomes the new subject. The verb turns into its past participle, and a matching form of "be" is added in front of it.

Direct → Indirect Speech The Backshift Rule

Drop the quotation marks, add "that", swap the pronoun to match the reporter, and push the tense one step into the past.

Degrees of Comparison -er/-est vs more/most

Short words change their tail. Long words get "more" and "most" in front instead. A handful of stubborn words just have to be memorised.

Singular → Plural Endings decide the rule

Hissing endings get "-es", a consonant-plus-y flips to "-ies", and a stubborn list of old English words refuses to follow any rule at all.

A or An Listen for the sound, not the letter

The choice depends on the sound the next word starts with, not the letter. That's why it's "an hour" but "a university".
